# Molecular background

## Classification

Breast tumours are heterogeneous. Intrinsic transcriptomic classes include Luminal A, Luminal B, HER2-enriched and basal-like; each is a pattern of gene expression, usually assigned with a defined classifier such as PAM50. Clinical immunohistochemistry (IHC) reports ER, PR and HER2 receptor status and often Ki-67, but the IHC categories are related surrogates rather than identical to intrinsic subtypes. In particular, ER positivity alone does not assign Luminal A, and a source label cannot be retroactively called a PAM50 result without the classifier and input specification ([Parker et al., 2009](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.2008.18.1370)). This dataset uses the depositing laboratory's Luminal A/B labels, whose independent assay criteria are incompletely documented in the matrix.

## Steroid receptor networks

ESR1 encodes ERα, a ligand-regulated transcription factor that binds chromatin with co-regulators and drives context-dependent oestrogen-response programs. PGR encodes nuclear PR isoforms; PR is often an ER-regulated marker but also modifies ER chromatin binding and transcription under progesterone exposure ([Mohammed et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14583)). Classical nuclear PR can regulate transcription directly or through paracrine outputs. Membrane-associated PR signalling, PGRMC1 and kinase cross-talk are reported in experimental systems, yet receptor identity and downstream effects vary by model; expression of PGRMC1 alone does not establish a progesterone signal ([Pedroza et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.3390/cancers12092440); [Hagan & Lange, 2014](https://doi.org/10.1186/1741-7015-12-32)).

## Cell cycle and growth factor cross-talk

Cyclin D1 (CCND1) complexes with CDK4/6, promotes RB phosphorylation and permits E2F-dependent cell-cycle transcription. This is a mechanistic basis for CDK4/6 inhibition in selected HR-positive disease. ER programmes and receptor tyrosine kinases can converge on PI3K/AKT/mTOR and MAPK/ERK, affecting growth, survival and endocrine response. FOXO1 is regulated by AKT-dependent localisation and other modifications; FOXO1 mRNA alone cannot establish FOXO1 activity. These mechanisms are not uniformly active across all labelled luminal tumours ([Finn et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13058-015-0661-5); [Ritchie et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkv007)).

In mammary experimental models, progesterone-induced RANKL (TNFSF11) can act on RANK (TNFRSF11A) in neighbouring cells, connecting PR-positive sensor cells to NF-κB-mediated proliferation. Progesterone-linked WNT4 is another proposed paracrine axis, with β-catenin effects depending on cell state and receptor context. Mouse and cell-model findings should not be generalised into evidence that all human tumours use the same circuit ([Beleut et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0915148107); [Schramek et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09387)). Neither ligand expression nor bulk tumour RNA establishes cell-to-cell signalling.

## Treatments and resistance

Tamoxifen is an ER modulator with tissue-specific effects. Fulvestrant promotes ER degradation. Anastrozole inhibits aromatase, reducing oestrogen synthesis in appropriate clinical settings. CDK4/6 inhibitors block cell-cycle progression in approved combinations and indications. Docetaxel stabilises microtubules and is cytotoxic irrespective of ER/PR targeting. These therapies are used in distinct clinical contexts, and no drug response can be predicted from this matrix alone ([NCI hormone therapy](https://www.cancer.gov/types/breast/treatment/hormone-therapy); [NCI drug list](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/drugs/breast)). Onapristone is a PR antagonist studied in a phase I setting; it is investigational for breast cancer, with no established standard-of-care role ([Cottu et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0204973)).

Acquired ESR1 ligand-binding-domain mutations provide a clinically supported mechanism of endocrine resistance in metastatic disease ([Toy et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.2822); [Robinson et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng.2823)). PI3K/AKT signalling, receptor tyrosine kinase cross-talk, altered cell-cycle control, changing ER programmes and clonal heterogeneity also contribute, but their evidence and therapeutic relevance vary by context. A primary-tumour transcriptome cannot establish acquired mutation status or resistance following therapy. Risk-factor topics from the group presentation are background only; this cohort has no individual risk-exposure measurements.
